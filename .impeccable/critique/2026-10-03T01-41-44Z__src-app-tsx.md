## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Authored for this product. Three things could not be lifted into another app: the sun is drawn in the net's own coordinate space, so dragging it toward a cluster brightens exactly those slopes; the loupe shows 8× unsmoothed DEM pixels with the chosen cell outlined; and the resting sun label speaks in the net labels' voice with a paper halo, so the light's position reads as part of the chart. The readout grammar and the caption's "5 m pixels · 288 × 294" are a geoscientist's. Generic: the title bar, the card-on-surface shell, the three-stat row, the dark paragraph tooltip, the error card. On a 1920px monitor the generic shell is most of what you see.

**Deterministic scan:** CLI detector over `src` (46 scannable files) and `index.html`, including `--scope layout`: 0 findings, exit 0 on all four runs; `--no-config` confirms nothing is suppressed and a bad control file returns 4 findings. The in-page detector (five injections: phone/desktop × first-open/selected, plus the sun under keyboard focus) reported the same single hit in every state: `cream-palette` on `rgb(247, 245, 241)` = `--surface: #f7f5f1` (`tokens.css:30`), the pinned design-system token. Correct but overridden by the brief; no action. No console errors or warnings on load.

**Visual overlays:** None user-visible; the detector ran headlessly and its banner is in the critique2/b screenshots.

**Against run 1:** every Priority Issue from run 1 is verifiably gone (Tab 1 lands on the terrain; the sun is named and announced; the label is raised then quiet; rings show through the shimmer; the readout sits under the net at 915px; the tablet stacks). The score is flat at 29 because the next layer surfaced: what the sun is for on a phone, what "flat" means on a net, and what a desk-sized window should do.

## Overall Impression

The link is still the product and still beautiful; the first touch on a phone remains the peak. The new weak spots cluster on two things. First, the sun on touch: it works, but nothing on a phone says it moves, and the tap hint from the last pass wastes its one chance by restating a value already on screen. Second, absences the geoscientist notices: the lake gives the net nothing back, the sun and the aspect speak different dialects, and once touched the screen has no way back to the clean portrait. The single biggest opportunity is the first.

## What's Working

1. The sun shares the data's coordinate system: the marker plots where a slope facing it would plot, so the control teaches the chart.
2. The loupe: 8× unsmoothed with the selected cell outlined removes the "which pixel?" doubt; placement held at every edge captured.
3. Accent discipline, now including the sun's state: rust only on the ring, the point, the loupe outline and the focus ring; the sun is neutral, and its position reads in the chart's own quiet voice.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The sun is invisible as a control on touch, and the tap hint at the default repeats what is already visible.**
- Why it matters: the positioning claim is "with the sun adjustable" and the primary device is a phone. There is no hover, the caption names only the terrain, and `SunHandle.tsx` `onPointerUp` raises `atDefault ? null : RESET_HINT`, so at the default the dark label says "NW · 45° high", which the resting label already says.
- Fix: at the default, raise "Drag to move the light" (keep "Double-tap to reset" off default). On a coarse pointer, let the first-open caption read "Drag on the terrain to inspect a pixel · drag the sun to re-light it", or give the disc one pulse when the cloud fades in.
- Suggested command: /impeccable onboard.

**[P2] A flat pixel gives the net nothing back.**
- Why it matters: flat is the center (`toNet(0, α)` is the origin), not an absence. The lake is the most conspicuous feature on GORE; the readout answers ("0.0°", "Flat") but the point vanishes, which reads as "it broke". Spec §3 currently says the point fades out for flat pixels; this is an amendment.
- Fix: in `NetMarker.tsx`, for `surface.flat[index]` place the marker at the center as a hollow ring (accent stroke, paper fill) instead of `data-visible="false"`; keep no-data hidden. Amend spec §3.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify.

**[P2] The sun and the aspect readout speak different dialects.**
- Why it matters: "SSE · 67° high" (16-point, no number) cannot be matched against "147° SE"; the keyboard's 5° steps are invisible in the label.
- Fix: `formatSun` in `src/terrain/net.ts` → `"315° NW · 45° high"` (degrees first, 8-point letter, matching the readout). Label, name and announcement follow.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify.

**[P2] There is no exit from a selection.**
- Why it matters: once touched, the ring and point persist until reload; Escape closes a tooltip and nothing else; with one DEM there is no switch to clear it.
- Fix: in `usePixelDrag.ts` `onKeyDown`, Escape sets the selection to −1 and announces "Selection cleared"; the caption returns to the hint.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden.

**[P2] The desktop layout stops at 520px.**
- Why it matters: "at a desk" is a confirmed context; at 1440×900 and 1920×1080 the terrain is 520×530 regardless, with 12px and 14px text as the only words.
- Fix: `layout.ts` `MAX_COLUMN` 520 → scale with the viewport (up to ~760 when `room` allows), keeping the net no wider than the terrain and the readout under the net.
- Suggested command: /impeccable layout.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power user):** Escape clears nothing; no Home/End on the terrain (center-to-corner is 15 Shift presses); the sun's exact azimuth cannot be read or typed; every Tab onto the sun opens the tooltip over the north cloud until a key is pressed; the info dot drops a seven-line paragraph over the net.

**Sam (screen reader / keyboard):** Tab order terrain → sun, both named; `role="application"` and the polite region work. Flags: the announcement never says where; an arrow at the grid's edge or a re-press of the same pixel is silent because the string is unchanged; the sun's name carries its instructions on every focus; the empty readout is three en dashes.

**Casey (one-handed phone, interrupted):** terrain in the thumb zone is right. Flags: the sun handle and the info dot are a regrip away at the top; a 300 ms double-tap window means a jittery second tap resets the light; a killed tab loses the place she was studying.

**Dana (hydrologist with a site in mind):** no scale bar, north cue or coordinates; the extent is only "288 × 294" after a touch; the sun's compass-only label forces conversion from the aspect she just read; the single DEM's name is a label, so nothing says a picker exists.

## Minor Observations

- The resting sun label after a drag can land over the dense SW/SE arc; 12px with a 3px paper stroke is marginal there. Pick the side with less ink, or thicken the stroke to 4px.
- The net help is three sentences in a 260px tooltip; on desktop it overhangs the terrain card.
- The hint never returns after the first press; "Shift moves ten" is lost after the first arrow.
- Readout values (18px/600) equal the app name in size; on desktop the answer is the quietest text on screen.
- The same phone shows two layouts with and without browser chrome (915px vs 839px). Both fit.
- Verified fits with no overflow: 640×400, 375×667, sideways, 720×900, 768×1024, 1024×768.
- Could not verify: real-phone drag feel, safe-area insets, actual NVDA/VoiceOver output, Safari/Firefox.
- Cognitive load: 1 marginal failure of 8 (visual hierarchy). Low load.

## Questions to Consider

1. The sun already lives in the net's coordinates, so why does pressing elsewhere on the net do nothing? Tapping anywhere on the net to put the light there is the most natural gesture on the screen and the one the spec forbids.
2. What if the link ran both ways — brush the cloud and the terrain shows which pixels plot there?
3. Is "Flat" an absence or a place? Water is the hydrologist's feature; the center of the net is its home.
4. Is a hillshade without a scale bar, a north cue or an extent a place? What is the one line the caption must say so Dana can find her reach?
5. On a phone the light control is at the top and the thumb at the bottom. Could a long-press-then-slide on the terrain itself set the light?
