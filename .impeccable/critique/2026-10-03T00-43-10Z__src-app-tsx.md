## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Authored for this product. The composition is the thesis — one card you touch, one that answers, with rust reserved for the single correspondence between them (ring, point, loupe outline). The loupe lifting above the finger, the point that springs and pulses once, the sun as a neutral disc placed on the net where a slope facing it would plot, "259° W" and "ENE · 35° high" as copy, the Flat / No data states: none of it is transferable. What is generic is the chrome: a title bar spending 56px on an app name and an inert DEM label, four Lucide `(i)` dots that are a design-system reflex rather than a product decision, and a centered-flex stage that lets leftover space pool at the top and bottom of a tall phone. The identity lives entirely in the two cards.

## Overall Impression

The link works and it is beautiful: press, and loupe, ring, point and three numbers arrive in the same frame. Color discipline is exemplary. The weaknesses cluster around one thing — the sun is a control without a state. Its value exists only while a finger is down, is never spoken to assistive tech, and the only way to reset it is a gesture nothing on screen names. For the confirmed user, a hydrologist re-lighting a site for a figure, "sun adjustable" is half the positioning claim, and the screen never states the illumination it shows. The single biggest opportunity: make the sun a first-class readout, with the handle merely the way to change it.

## What's Working

1. The correspondence is real and legible. Spring tuning (`POINT_SPRING` 300/32) keeps the point followable in a dense cloud; the once-only pulse makes it findable; loupe placement is correct at the top edge and in the corners.
2. Color discipline. Rust only on the correspondence; the sun stays neutral; the cloud is ink. Nothing competes with the selection, so the eye never hunts.
3. The keyboard path exists and shows itself. Arrows on the terrain with a visible 3px ring, arrows/Home on the sun with the ring on the disc, `role="application"` with instructions in its name, and a polite live region that announces on settle, not per frame.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The sun's state is invisible at rest and silent to assistive tech.**
- Why it matters: `.sun__label` is bound to `dragging` (`SunHandle.tsx:205`), so keyboard steps show nothing and the value vanishes when the finger lifts. The handle is a plain `button` with a static `aria-label` and no `aria-valuetext`; a screen reader hears "Sun. Arrow keys move the light" and never a value, while the hidden span leaks as stray text. A hydrologist cannot state or reproduce the illumination in a screenshot.
- Fix: add `aria-roledescription="sun"` and `aria-valuetext` from the same formatter the drag label uses; show the label on focus and for ~1.5 s after each key step; print the setting at rest as a quiet fourth item ("Sun NW · 45°") in the readout or caption.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden, then /impeccable clarify.

**[P1] Wide mode: focus order contradicts visual order; the terrain is the sixth Tab stop.**
- Why it matters: `App.tsx` renders `NetCard` before `terrain-col` and `app.css:85` flips it with `order: -1`. Tab goes net-info → sun → three readout dots → terrain, and the very first Tab opens a four-line tooltip over the net. Keyboard and screen-reader users reach the primary control last, from the wrong side of the screen (WCAG 2.4.3).
- Fix: render the terrain column first in the DOM when `layout.mode === 'wide'`; make the four `(i)` dots `tabIndex={-1}` with their text attached via `aria-describedby`, so the path is terrain → sun.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden.

**[P2] Double-tap reset is undiscoverable on touch, the primary platform.**
- Why it matters: the sun tooltip opens on hover/focus only; `onPointerDown` closes it and Radix never opens on touch. A sun dragged to 10° at the rim leaves the terrain near-black (verified), and nothing on screen says how to recover. A workaround exists (drag it back), which keeps this out of P1.
- Fix: on a tap with no movement, show the label with "double-tap to reset" for 2 s; or show a small tonal "Reset sun" text button in the net card whenever the sun is off default.
- Suggested command: /impeccable onboard / /impeccable clarify.

**[P2] The layout starves the net on tall phones and both cards in tablet-portrait windows.**
- Why it matters: at 412×915 the net is capped at 276px by the 76px side readout (`layout.ts:41`) while ~64px of empty surface sits above the net card and below the caption (verified). At 720×900, `WIDE_FROM = 720` forces wide mode by width alone: terrain 348px, net 245px, roughly 60% of the viewport empty (verified). The net is the "portrait of the landscape", and it is the thing being shrunk.
- Fix: in portrait, when `free − terrainHeight ≥ netMax + READOUT_BELOW`, move the readout below the net and give the net the card width (364px at 412 wide, +32%); choose wide vs portrait by whichever maximizes `min(netSize, terrainWidth)`, not by width.
- Suggested command: /impeccable layout.

**[P3] Four persistent `(i)` dots and a decorative title bar for an expert audience.**
- Why it matters: the confirmed users know slope, aspect and elevation; the three dots in `Readout.tsx` are noise in every state and three extra Tab stops. `.titlebar__dem` (600 weight) looks like a picker and does nothing.
- Fix: keep the net's `(i)` and fold the three definitions into it; set the single-DEM name to 400 weight in `--ink-500`.
- Suggested command: /impeccable quieter.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power user):** five Tab stops before the terrain in wide mode; Tab 1 opens a tooltip across the net. No numeric entry for the sun — "from 180° at 30°" means dragging and watching a label that then vanishes. Shift+arrow ×10 and Home exist but are documented nowhere visible. With a mouse the core task takes under ten seconds, so Alex stays, annoyed.

**Sam (screen reader / keyboard):** the sun button has no value semantics; its hidden label is read as unassociated text; keyboard moves of the sun produce no visible or spoken value. Terrain-last focus order on desktop. What works: `role="application"` on the terrain with instructions in its name, the live region on settle, 3px focus rings, no scroll at 640×400 (200%-equivalent).

**Casey (one-handed phone, interrupted):** the terrain sits in thumb reach, good. The sun lives at the top of the screen (y≈220) and demands a double-tap up there one-handed. A reclaimed tab comes back with the default sun and no selection, silently; re-selecting is one touch, but the sun setting is lost.

**Priya (hydrologist with a site in mind — from PRODUCT.md):** she cannot find her site: no scale bar, no north arrow, and the caption says "5 m pixels · 288 × 294" instead of "1.4 × 1.5 km". Slope is degrees only; she works in percent as often. The sun setting she chose for a figure is printed nowhere, so her screenshot has no illumination caption. The lake reads as a gray blob until touched.

## Minor Observations

- The loading skeleton (`.net__skeleton`, inset 3.125%) covers the 30°/60° rings and the cross; the spec's "net frame drawn" and polish item 2 are only partly met.
- The net's `(i)` tooltip opens upward on a phone and covers the app name; give that one `side="bottom"`.
- The sun disc at the E rim hides the "E" and "60°" labels (verified).
- With two DEMs the segmented control is 52px in a 56px bar; 2px of breathing room.
- At 375×667 the terrain (337px) and net card (351px) no longer share an edge.
- Copy varies by modality but the UI does not: "Drag…" for keyboard users, "double-tap" for mouse users.
- Cognitive load: 1 of 8 checklist failures (working memory — the sun setting vanishes on release). Low load; no decision point exceeds 4 options.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the net is the portrait of the landscape, why does a 76px readout column get to shrink it by a third on every phone? What if the three numbers sat in the net square's empty corners?
2. What if the sun were a first-class readout ("Sun NW · 45°") and the handle merely the way to change it, so the screen always states the illumination it shows?
3. The app knows cell size and dimensions; why withhold the one multiplication the user needs — ground extent, a scale bar?
4. The hint is the smallest text on the screen. What if first open ghost-pressed the center pixel for a second so the gesture demonstrates itself and no sentence is needed?
5. A tap and a drag do the same thing. Could a tap pin and a drag preview, so a reader can compare two places without area selection?
